Slide 3: Problem Statement

Bridging Machine Learning Theory with Real-Time Critical Systems

Continuous Weather Temperature Forecasting

Accurate ambient temperature prediction is critical for urban heatwave early warning, solar grid integration, and agricultural scheduling. Weather features exhibit complex diurnal and thermodynamic non-linearities requiring continuous, sub-degree regression algorithms.

Multi-Class Disaster Hazard Classification

Natural catastrophes stem from disparate physical mechanisms (Hydrological floods vs. Meteorological cyclones vs. Geophysical earthquakes). Rapidly categorizing the primary environmental hazard type from early spatio-temporal indicators enables civil defense authorities to allocate emergency resources effectively.

Slide 11: Comparative Algorithmic Analysis

Bias-Variance Tradeoffs and Computational Efficiency

Why SVR and Random Forest Outperformed Others

SVR in Regression: The $\epsilon$-insensitive loss function ignores small atmospheric turbulence below $0.1^\circ\text{C}$, fitting a smooth, high-margin continuous boundary that generalizes with near-zero error.

Random Forest in Classification: Disaster occurrences follow strict non-linear physical rules (cyclones require ocean warmth; droughts require prolonged duration). Random Forest naturally uncovers orthogonal decision thresholds while bagging dampens sample variance.

Underperformance of Linear Models in Disaster Classification

Logistic Regression attained only $59.51\%$ accuracy because geographical coordinates and multi-hazard interactions cannot be partitioned by planar linear hyperplanes.
